What happened

The rule, stated plainly

  • Below standing and under ~3 offers a week for two consecutive weeks → flagged.
  • Next suitable callout in range routes to them first, ignoring rank. One per week, capped.
  • That offer gets the old 90s window, not 60 — they're cold, not unwilling.
  • Taken → normal credit, and they re-enter the ranking honestly. Missed → no extra penalty, but the next look is the week after, not the same day.
  • Handler and responder both see it coming. Nadia's team can see it on a ticket.

What does standing mean?

plain-language explainer

How Dispatch decides who to ask first

Every available responder gets a score for each callout. Highest score is asked first; if they don't say yes, we move down the list. Three things go into it:

60%  how close they are  — was 45% before 4.2
25%  how often they've been accepting lately  — was 40%
15%  whether they can do this kind of work  — unchanged since 4.0

Closeness isn't pass/fail — it falls off steadily with travel time and hits zero at 45 minutes. Five minutes out and forty minutes out are very different scores.

Standing is the middle one

Everyone starts at 0.5. Accept a callout and it goes up 0.08. Turn one down — or don't answer in time — and it goes down 0.12. Those two are currently scored identically, which is the defect engineering is fixing.

Why it doesn't come back on its own

There is no decay. Standing only rises when someone accepts an offer — and a low standing means fewer offers arrive. Fewer offers, fewer chances to recover, and any rare offer is likelier to lapse in 60 seconds anyway. It is a one-way trap, and nothing in the system reverses it.

What 4.2 changed

Closeness started counting more and standing less, and the answer window went from 90 to 60 seconds. Nobody's standing was reset at the release — whatever score a responder was carrying carried straight over.

This panel is the routing documentation that doesn't exist. There is no written spec for how ranking decides who gets pinged — it's a known open item. Half the value of building this screen is that it forces one to exist.
